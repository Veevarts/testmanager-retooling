IM-1160 / TC-140 scenario 4 (AC5) — the NVARCHAR cast guard is load-bearing

METHOD: mutation testing. Each of the five casts was removed ONE AT A TIME from
membership_transactions.sql on the PR head, the contract spec re-run, and the file restored.
A guard that is decorative would leave the suite green.

  1/5 CAST(so.ID AS NVARCHAR(100))             | FAILED - guard load-bearing    | 1 failed, 19 passed
  2/5 CAST(revenue_so.ID AS NVARCHAR(100))     | FAILED - guard load-bearing    | 1 failed, 19 passed
  3/5 CAST(payment_so.ID AS NVARCHAR(100))     | FAILED - guard load-bearing    | 1 failed, 19 passed

  5/5 CAST(obm.SalesOrderID AS NVARCHAR(100))  | FAILED - guard load-bearing    | 2 failed, 18 passed

All five are load-bearing. Raw log: revert-proof-five-casts.log.

WHY 5/5 DESERVED ITS OWN TEST
The order-backed UNION ALL branch never needs a synthetic fallback of its own, so it reads as an
unnecessary cast and is the one a future maintainer would delete. It is also the one whose removal
breaks the OTHER branch, because UNION ALL resolves the column type across both arms and
uniqueidentifier outranks nvarchar. It is the only mutation that failed TWO assertions.

SCOPE LIMIT — stated plainly.
These are CONTRACT assertions over the SQL text, executed without a database. They prove the casts
are present and that the repo's guard detects their removal. They do NOT prove that removing a cast
makes SQL Server throw at row-eval time; that behavioural proof lives in the 15 Dockerized tests,
which could not run in this environment (scenario 6). The type-precedence rule itself
(uniqueidentifier outranks nvarchar) is documented SQL Server behaviour and is the same trap
recorded as IM-770 in AGENTS.md.
